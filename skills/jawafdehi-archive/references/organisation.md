# About Jawafdehi

Background for answering questions about the organisation itself rather than about a case.
Live case, entity and court data comes from the tools, never from this file — and this
file deliberately carries **no counts and no case names**, because both go stale and both
are answerable live.

## What Jawafdehi is

Jawafdehi is a civic-technology platform that documents corruption and accountability in
Nepal. It takes corruption cases — principally those filed by the Commission for the
Investigation of Abuse of Authority (CIAA) — and turns the official record into a
structured, searchable, plain-language archive that anyone can read and check. Each case
links the people and organisations involved, the alleged conduct, the dates, the court
proceedings, and the source documents behind every claim.

It is built and run by Nepali volunteers in Nepal and across the diaspora, alongside a
small paid team of intern lawyers and engineers. It is free to use and carries no
advertising.

Jawafdehi is not a court, a government body, a law firm or any kind of legal authority. It
does not prosecute, adjudicate or give legal advice. It documents the public record and
makes it legible.

The platform is bilingual. Case content is primarily in Nepali, with English available
throughout.

## What the name means

*Jawafdehi* (जवाफदेही) is Nepali for **accountability** — being answerable for one's
actions. The related everyday noun is *jawafdehita* (जवाफदेहिता). The organisation's full
name is **Jawafdehi Initiative** (जवाफदेही इनिशिएटिभ); the wordmark uses the single word
जवाफदेही.

## How Jawafdehi is funded

Jawafdehi is a product by Nepalis, for Nepal. It is volunteer-led and takes no profit. It
accepts **no money from any foreign government or foreign funder**, and none from any
government or political party. Its support comes from ordinary Nepali people and from
small Nepali businesses that share the mission — including donated infrastructure and
services.

Money goes primarily to **Nepali students interning at Jawafdehi** — the people who
research, verify and maintain the archive — along with the hosting and tooling that keep
the platform running and free.

Jawafdehi's finances and operations are transparent. Anyone who wants detail can write to
**inquiry@jawafdehi.org** and ask.

Gifts to the US entity, Jawafdehi Initiative Inc., are tax-deductible for US taxpayers to
the extent allowed by law (EIN 42-3114954). Jawafdehi makes no claim that gifts are
deductible under Nepali tax law.

## What Jawafdehi documents

The core of the archive is corruption cases filed by the **CIAA** — Nepal's constitutional
anti-corruption body — and prosecuted before the **Special Court** (विशेष अदालत), with
appeals to the Supreme Court. Each case is indexed with its charge sheet, court orders,
verdicts and supporting records.

Jawafdehi is not CIAA-exclusive. It also documents major cases of national public interest
that fall outside CIAA filings — large tax and telecoms disputes, land and privatisation
matters, and cooperative and media-financing scandals among them — drawing on court
records, the Attorney General's Office, official government documents and credible media
reporting. To find which specific cases of this kind are in the archive, **search it**; do
not rely on remembered examples.

The forms of corruption tracked are the standard ones: **bribery** (घुस) — giving or
soliciting something of value to influence an official; **embezzlement** (अपचय) —
misappropriation of funds held in trust; **nepotism and favouritism** in hiring and
contracting; **conflict of interest**; **abuse of public resources and authority**; and
related offences such as forged documents and fraudulent certification. Corruption itself
is defined as the abuse of entrusted power for private gain.

Alongside corruption cases the archive carries other case types where the public interest
is clear, such as tax evasion.

## What the archive contains

Four kinds of record. **Never state a count of any of them from this file** — every figure
is deliberately omitted because it changes daily. Get the current number from the tools.

- **Published Jawafdehi cases** — curated, human-reviewed accountability records. Mostly
  corruption cases, plus other types such as tax evasion. Each carries a status: closed,
  ongoing, or other.
- **Entities** — the people, organisations, government bodies, courts and places that
  appear in cases, each with a stable identifier so the same person can be followed across
  cases.
- **Materials** — the underlying documents: court orders, official reports, news articles,
  charge sheets, precedents, procurement notices and press releases.
- **Court cases** indexed for public search, drawn from the district courts, High Courts,
  the Special Court and the Supreme Court.

A **Jawafdehi case** is a curated, human-reviewed accountability record. A **court case**
is a raw record from Nepal's court system — most of that docket is ordinary civil and
criminal litigation that has nothing to do with corruption. A court record appearing in
the archive is not an allegation of corruption and must never be described as one.

## How a case is verified and published

Every Jawafdehi case goes through the same process before it is published.

1. **Source collection** — government records, court documents, CIAA filings, media
   reporting, NGO investigations and credible accounts are gathered.
2. **Cross-verification** — claims must be corroborated. The standard is a minimum of two
   independent credible sources per allegation, with official documentation wherever it
   exists.
3. **Team review** — the moderation team reviews each case for factual accuracy, legal
   compliance and ethical standards before approval.
4. **Publication** — the case goes live with every factual claim linked back to its
   primary source.
5. **Continuous updates** — cases are monitored and updated as proceedings move, verdicts
   land, appeals are filed, and accused parties respond.

Throughout, Jawafdehi keeps a clear distinction between **verified fact**, **allegation**,
and **ongoing investigation**. Someone charged is not someone convicted, and the archive
says which is which.

AI is used — including Anthropic's Claude — to process and summarise large volumes of
legal documents quickly. Every AI-generated case summary is reviewed by a human for
factual accuracy before publication. Speed does not substitute for review. Anthropic's
policies specify that Jawafdehi's data is not used to train their models.

## Right to respond, and corrections

Any individual or organisation named in a case has a **right to respond**. There is no
self-serve submission portal for this; an accused party should contact the Jawafdehi team
directly. Identity is verified first through established official channels — an official
social media account, or a video call — and once verified, the response is published
alongside the original allegation so readers see the full context.

Errors happen. Anyone who finds inaccurate information should contact Jawafdehi through
the feedback form with supporting evidence. Verified inaccuracies are corrected promptly,
and corrections are documented in the case's audit trail rather than quietly overwritten.

## Privacy and safety

Jawafdehi practises minimal data collection. Submissions are voluntary and can be made
without a name or email. Access to submissions is limited to authorised personnel.
Personal information is never sold, rented or shared for commercial purposes.

A source's identity is never published without their informed consent. Personal data such
as phone numbers and national identity numbers is not published. Anyone concerned about
their safety should leave identifying details out of a submission, and may wish to use
privacy tools such as the Tor Browser.

The archive is about public accountability, not private individuals. Ordinary people
appearing in routine court records — family, property, or other private matters — are not
subjects of accountability reporting.

**Case data** is published under **Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 (CC
BY-NC 4.0)**. Anyone may read, share and build on it for non-commercial purposes —
journalism, research, education, advocacy — with credit to Jawafdehi.org. Commercial use
requires a separate licence, which carries ethical-use terms; write to
inquiry@jawafdehi.org. The data is deliberately **not** placed in the public domain, so

**Software** is published under the **Hippocratic License 3.0**, an ethical-source licence
that restricts use for human-rights abuses. It is not an OSI-approved open-source licence.
The public repositories include the Jawafdehi API, the case reviewers and graders, and
likhit, the Nepali document-to-Markdown converter. Some internal tooling remains private.

## How to report a corruption case

Use the **"Report a case" form** on jawafdehi.org, with as much detail and supporting
documentation as possible. Reports may be submitted **anonymously or with identity
attached**. Every submission is reviewed before anything is published, and Jawafdehi may
follow up for verification if contact details were left.

Other channels: **report@jawafdehi.org**, or **WhatsApp +977 9768630501**.

After submission, the report enters the verification queue. The team reviews it,
corroborates it across sources, and decides whether it meets publication standards. **This
typically takes 7–14 days**, depending on complexity. Anyone who left contact details is
notified of the outcome.

To go straight to the authorities instead, corruption can be reported to the **CIAA** —
hotline **107**, toll-free **1660-01-22233**, or **ujuri@ciaa.gov.np** — or to
**Transparency International Nepal** at
transparency.org/en/report-corruption/nepal.

## How to support Jawafdehi

**Financially.** Support is accepted via PayPal and international bank wire (SWIFT) to the
US entity, and domestically in Nepal through the Nepal entity. Contributions primarily
fund employment — the researchers and verifiers who build the archive — plus hosting and
tooling. Gifts to Jawafdehi Initiative Inc. (501(c)(3), EIN 42-3114954) are tax-deductible
for US taxpayers to the extent allowed by law. For donation questions, write to
inquiry@jawafdehi.org.

**By volunteering.** No expertise is required — what is needed is care for Nepal and a
willingness to contribute a skill. Volunteers work on data scraping, outreach, platform
development, case compilation, corruption research, translation, design, documentation and
public-records review. Technologists, working professionals, legal minds and students are
all welcome, in Nepal or anywhere in the diaspora. Email to introduce yourself, or join
the community WhatsApp group.
